# Code of conduct

This project is about disks, images and the code that handles them. The rules
below exist so that it stays about that.

## Scope

Everything that happens in the project's spaces: issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, commit messages, documentation, and any chat the project runs. It
applies to maintainers, contributors and visitors alike.

## 1. Keep it about the work

The project's spaces are for the project. Politics, ideologies, religion, activism
and campaigns of any kind, for or against anything, do not belong here, however
important they may be elsewhere. That includes slogans, badges and banners in
profiles, signatures, commit messages, code comments and documentation. Nobody has
to declare, defend or avoid any view to take part; the only thing judged here is
the contribution.

## 2. Argue the code, not the person

Disagreement is normal and useful. Blunt technical criticism of a patch, a design
or a decision is welcome ("this leaks the handle on the error path", "this layout
breaks on 4 KiB sectors"). Insults, ridicule, name-calling, guesses about someone's
competence, motives or character, and remarks about who someone is rather than what
they wrote are not. If you cannot make the point about the work, do not make it.

## 3. Be straight

Say what you mean, show the evidence, and accept being shown wrong. Report what the
tests did, not what you hoped they would do. If you review, review the change in
front of you.

## Enforcement

Maintainers keep the spaces to these rules. Off-topic material is removed; a
personal attack earns a warning and the content is removed; a repeat, or anything
that clearly meant harm, ends participation. There is no appeal process beyond
writing to the maintainer, and the maintainer's decision stands.

## Reporting

If someone breaks these rules and a maintainer has not seen it, write to the
maintainer directly (see the repository's owner on GitHub). Reports are read and
answered, and are not published.
